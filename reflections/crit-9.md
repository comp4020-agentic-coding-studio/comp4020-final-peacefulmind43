# Crit 9 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough came from a number that looked too good. I added the deflated
Sharpe ratio to discount results for luck, and every test passed. Then I ran it
over real rules and every rule scored 100%. Nothing was broken in the code. The
question was wrong: it asked whether a momentum rule beats cash, and over 89
years every one does. The question I actually care about, as someone who holds
SPMO, is whether a rule adds anything beyond holding more or less of it. Once I
changed the question to that, the scores spread out, and the room started to
show what it was built for: the same rule looks less convincing as the room
tries more rules.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I used to think that if the tests pass, the work is right. This week showed me
that tests only check what I already thought to ask. A correct formula can
still answer the wrong question, and only looking at real output caught it. I
want to be a developer who checks whether a result makes sense, not only
whether it passes, and who writes that habit down so the next session, mine or
the agent's, does it too.
