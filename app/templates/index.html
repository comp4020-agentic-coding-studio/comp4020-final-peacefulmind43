How a test works

At the end of each month, the rule decides how much to hold next month, using only what was known then. Money not in the portfolio earns the one-month US Treasury bill rate; borrowed money costs that rate plus 1.5% a year. Holding the portfolio costs 1% a year, and changing how much you hold costs 0.1% of the amount moved. These costs are rough allowances, not measurements. If the money ever runs out, it stays at zero.

The portfolio is the top 10% of US stocks by their return over the previous year (skipping the last month), weighted by company size. It is similar in spirit to a momentum fund, but it is not any real fund.

"Chance it beats luck" is the deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and López de Prado, 2014): the probability that a rule's true Sharpe ratio is above zero, after allowing for how many different rules this room has tried, how much their results differ, and how lopsided and fat-tailed the returns are.
